# 12 — Tactic Design Rules

## What Makes a Good Tactic?

A tactic is the atomic unit of execution. Bad tactics destroy plans. Good tactics make execution automatic.

## The 6 Tactic Design Rules

### 1. Fully Controllable

The user must be able to complete the tactic through their own actions alone. It must not depend on:
- Someone else responding
- Luck or timing
- External approval

| ❌ Bad | ✅ Good |
|--------|---------|
| "Get a promotion" | "Schedule 2 meetings with my manager to discuss growth" |
| "Get 100 customers" | "Send 10 outreach DMs per day" |
| "Lose 5 kg" | "Track every meal in the app and complete 4 workouts per week" |

### 2. Binary Completion

The tactic must be scored as done or not done. Ambiguous completion invites negotiation.

| ❌ Bad | ✅ Good |
|--------|---------|
| "Work on the project" | "Write 500 words for the report" |
| "Study" | "Complete 1 quiz in the course" |
| "Network" | "Send 2 LinkedIn connection requests with personalized notes" |

### 3. Known Time Block

The tactic must fit into a specific, estimated duration. If the user cannot guess how long it takes, they will not schedule it.

| ❌ Bad | ✅ Good |
|--------|---------|
| "Read" | "Read 1 chapter (approx. 25 min)" |
| "Clean" | "Tidy the kitchen for 10 minutes" |

### 4. Minimum Effective Dose (MED)

The smallest version of the tactic that still produces progress. Start smaller than comfortable. You can always increase.

| Too Big | MED |
|---------|-----|
| 60-minute workout | 10-minute walk |
| Write 2,000 words/day | Write 250 words/day |
| 30-minute meditation | 2-minute breathing exercise |

### 5. Tied to Goal and Vision

Every tactic must clearly connect backward to the goal and the vision. If you cannot explain how this action matters, delete it.

Test: "Doing [tactic] moves me toward [goal] because... which moves me toward [vision] because..."

### 6. Scheduled Frequency

The tactic must have a known cadence:
- **Daily:** e.g., "Morning journal, 5 min"
- **Weekly:** e.g., "Publish 1 blog post"
- **One-time:** e.g., "Register domain by week 2"

## Tactic Types

| Type | Best For | Example |
|------|----------|---------|
| **Daily habit** | Identity change, momentum | "Write 500 words every morning" |
| **Weekly deliverable** | Output-based goals | "Publish 1 piece of content" |
| **One-time action** | Setup, infrastructure | "Buy gym membership" |
| **Review / measurement** | Feedback loops | "Weigh in every Friday" |

## Tactic Count Guidelines

| User Experience | Max Tactics per Week | Max Tactics per Day |
|-----------------|----------------------|----------------------|
| Beginner | 5 total | 1–2 |
| Intermediate | 8 total | 2–3 |
| Advanced | 12 total | 3–4 |

**Rule:** If the user has ever failed a habit plan before, treat them as a beginner.

## Common Tactic Redesigns

| Weak Tactic | Diagnosis | Redesigned |
|-------------|-----------|------------|
| "Eat healthier" | Vague, not binary | "Eat protein at every meal" — daily check |
| "Be more productive" | No time block, not controllable | "Protect 9–11 AM as no-meeting deep work" — daily |
| "Get better at sales" | Lag measure, not controllable | "Ask 5 discovery questions per call" — per call |
| "Build a website" | Too big | "Buy domain + set up hosting" (week 1), "Write homepage copy" (week 2), etc. |

## Agent Tactic Review Checklist

When reviewing a user's tactics, verify each one:

- [ ] Is it fully within the user's control?
- [ ] Is it binary (done / not done)?
- [ ] Does it have an estimated duration?
- [ ] Is it the minimum effective dose?
- [ ] Can the user explain how it links to their goal and vision?
- [ ] Is it scheduled on a specific day at a specific time?

If any box is unchecked, redesign the tactic before the user leaves the conversation.
